        Two ways to run a junction

        You just built a give-way junction — the free default. Here's what your other two options actually look like in motion, and when to reach for each.

        Roundabout

        Several cars circulate at once, and almost nobody has to stop completely — traffic just slots in and flows round. Use it where four or more busy roads genuinely meet, especially once a give-way there is visibly backing up. It costs more than a give-way and needs three or more roads to make sense.

        Traffic light

        Sensor-driven: whichever side has cars gets the green, and when both sides have cars they take turns. Click a placed light to set exactly how many cars each side gets per turn — 3:3 by default, or a lopsided ratio like 20:3 for a busy main road crossing a quiet side street. Use it once a junction gets busy from more than one direction at once — it's calmer and cheaper than a roundabout, just slower per car.

        Bridges & motorways

        Bridges let ordinary road cross water — drag across a river the same way you'd drag anywhere else, and a bridge is laid automatically wherever the drag touches water. They just cost a little more per tile than plain road, and you'll need one in stock (or the cash for it) to lay it.

        Motorways are an express link between two distant road tiles, six times the speed of ordinary road. Only cars with a bigger fuel tank can use them. They're expensive and only connect two points directly — no on-ramps in between — so they're built for long hauls across a big, spread-out city, not for stitching together local streets.

        Signs

        Signs are read from the driver's seat, not the map: a blue arrow means that's the only move allowed from this tile; a red ring bans one specific move. The same Signs tool also has a one-way option — pick it, then click a plain two-way stretch (not a junction) to make it flow one direction only; click again to flip it, and once more for two-way. Making a tight corridor one-way is free and removes a whole class of conflict from it.

        Common questions

        Why isn't my new house or store doing anything?

        It isn't connected yet. A house or bay only works once a road tile is laid on one of its four sides — drag a road into it, or drag out from it. A store is a 2×2 building with a small car park along one side. The road has to reach an open end of that car park (marked with an arrow): some stores open at both ends, others at only one. Roads alongside the car park don’t connect.

        Why did my city end?

        Either one store's parcels overflowed for too long, or the whole network gridlocked for a sustained stretch even with no single store overflowing. If you'd rather build with no losing condition at all, Zen difficulty removes it entirely.

        Give-way, light or roundabout — what's the real difference?

        A give-way is free and fine for light traffic. A light suits a junction that's busy from more than one side. A roundabout suits a junction that's busy from three or more sides at once — it costs more but almost never makes anyone stop outright.

        Why do prices keep going up?

        Road, signs, lights, roundabouts, lots, bays and motorways all rise 15% a week, compounding — so it pays to build key pieces early. Past a free allowance, existing road also costs a small amount of upkeep every week.

        What happens when a store "tiers up"?

        It randomly grows busier over time, in up to three steps — a thicker outline means it now sends out parcels faster and pays a little more per parcel, and two new houses of its colour move in nearby to match the extra demand.

        Can I undo a mistake?

        Yes — Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y redoes. Both work back through everything from your last press-and-drag.

        Can I change the colours?

        Yes. Open Settings. Under Appearance, pick an interface theme, or choose your own colours for menus, buttons and highlights, and switch the panels between Clean, Glass and Road sign styles. Under Colour mode, pick red–green safe, blue–yellow safe, high contrast, or custom city colours you choose one by one. You can also switch on a shape for each colour on houses and stores.

        What is Expert Survival?

        A new seeded city every week (Monday to Sunday, UTC). Everyone gets the same map, the same first store and house and the same early events, so runs are directly comparable. It's tougher than Frantic. Your best run on the week's seed goes on the Expert leaderboard, labelled with the week's dates, the seed's name and its code. Tap any player there to see their city — who placed what — and their stats, and to send them a message. Messages live under Account → Messages, and the speech-bubble button on the top bar shows when you have new ones.

        What are Junc Bucks (◎) and stars (✦)?

        A currency for looks. You earn 1 for every 2 parcels delivered, in any city and any difficulty, and keep them between cities. The Store restocks every hour with 6 items (themes, maps, decorations and designs, ◎500–1000) and 6 colours (bought, or hired for 3 hours for ◎150). One special item a day costs ✦ (rare bucks) instead, and ✦ only come from Frantic cities: one for every parcel. Mystery crates (colour ◎150, item ◎200, object ◎250, three of each an hour) always hold something you don't own, and are the only way to get unique items. Everything you own is under Owned, and the palette button next to Settings opens a quick Customise panel. The standard light and dark looks and the colour-blind colour modes are always free.

        What's a tow truck actually for?

        Hired at a store, it drives out to stuck or broken-down cars nearby, hooks them, hauls them clear and saves their parcels — handy once a city is busy enough that a single stuck car can back up a whole street.

        Is a bigger city always a better city?

        No — score comes from steady throughput, not size. A small, tightly controlled loop consistently out-earns a sprawling network that's constantly jammed.

        Is there a "correct" way to play?

        No. There's no correct way to play Junction — take any path you want.

        How to Play

        Everything you need to take a city from a single crossroads to a network that runs itself.

        The goal

        Every parcel a car carries from a store to a matching-colour house scores a point and pays cash. Score comes from steady throughput, not sheer size — a small, well-controlled loop will consistently out-earn a sprawling network full of stop-start traffic.

        Laying road

        Drag to lay road. Roads only connect where you actually draw the link: two tiles sitting side by side stay separate until a drag runs between them, and dragging onto or through an existing road joins it. A slanted drag lays a true diagonal, corner to corner — diagonals can't cross each other. A house, store or bay attaches to whichever road tile you drag into its side and turns to face it.

        Traffic control — pick the right tool

        A junction is only as good as the control you give it. Junction hands you six ways to shape one, from free to expensive:

        • Give-way (free, default). The simplest junction: lets a batch of cars through from one side, then hands over to the next side with traffic waiting. Fine until it gets busy.
        • Traffic light. Sensor-driven — a side with cars gets green, and when both sides have cars they take turns. Click a placed light to set exactly how many cars each side gets per turn: 3:3 by default, or any ratio you like, such as 20:3.
        • Roundabout. Several cars circulate at once and almost nobody has to stop. The best fix for a genuinely busy meeting of four or more roads.
        • Turn signs. Restrict what a driver may do at a tile — only left, no right, and so on — read from the driver's seat, not the map.
        • One-way roads. Found inside the Signs tool. Turns a plain two-way stretch into one direction only, for free, removing a whole class of conflict from a tight corridor. Click again to flip it, and once more for two-way.
        • Motorway. An express link between two distant road tiles at six times the speed of ordinary road — built for long hauls across the city, not local streets.
        • Fuel. A stock car can drive 16 road tiles to a store and can't use motorways. Click a car and buy a Fuel tank: the first adds range to 32 tiles and opens motorways, the second goes any distance. Houses only send cars on trips they can make.

        Every give-way junction, light and roundabout can also be upgraded with cash for more throughput per level — click it and look for the upgrade panel.

        Houses, stores & growth

        Houses send cars out to fetch parcels; stores are where the parcels pile up. New houses and stores appear on their own timers as a city runs — but store activity feeds growth too: every new store that opens brings a house of its own colour with it, and whenever a store tiers up (gets busier and needs cars more often, from week 2 on), two more houses of that colour move in to match. A thicker outline on a store means a higher tier.

        Vehicles

        Every vehicle shows its parcel slots, filling as it loads. Click one to upgrade it. Carry size moves it up a model (hatchback, estate, pickup, panel van, box truck), adding a slot each time but making it 10% slower. Speed fits a faster kit (sport, GT, racer) for +12% each, so you can win the speed back. Loading cuts time at the store. Click a house to buy another car: each costs more than the last, and the house itself grows from cottage to family home to villa.

        Stores

        Each store takes up a 2×3 plot: a 2×2 building with a 1×2 car park along one side, where cars back in to load. Roads connect only at an open end of the car park, marked with an arrow: some stores open at both ends, some at only one. As a store tiers up it rebuilds: corner shop, supermarket, depot, then distribution centre. Each paid upgrade adds a rooftop sign, then solar panels, then flags and gold trim, and makes every parcel pay more.

        Money

        A parcel pays $2 at the door; upgrade a store (U) and it pays more, and busier stores and rush hour pay extra. Spend it on road, signs, lights and the Shop. Click any junction to upgrade it — each level lets more cars through. Hire a tow truck (Y, or the Shop tab) and it drives out from its store to hook stuck or broken cars, tow them clear and save their parcels. A council grant arrives each week, and you also pick one free reward: cash, pieces, or a free upgrade level. Road, sign, light, roundabout, lot, bay and motorway prices rise 15% every week, so build early. Past a free allowance, every extra tile of road also costs a small amount of upkeep each week — the weekly toast breaks down the grant, any upkeep and the current price rise.

        Events

        Rush hour multiplies parcels. Rain slows every car. Breakdowns block a lane until the driver gets going; a tow truck clears anything stuck for too long. From week 4, a busy stretch of road is sometimes coned off for half a minute — traffic finds another way, more than one stretch can close at once in a long-running city, and the city never cuts a house or store off completely. From week 3, an ambulance sometimes races from a house to a store; lights turn green for it as it comes, and it pays a bonus for arriving in time or costs you if it's late. From week 2, any store can randomly grow busier over time in up to three tiers, and can also flash a short-lived contract: collect enough parcels from it before its clock runs out for a cash bonus — missing one costs nothing but the bonus itself. Rush hour and rain can land on the same week and overlap. Even a city with no single store overflowing can still fail if the whole network grinds to a standstill for long enough. The Trouble spots list in the City tab always shows where traffic is standing longest — click one to jump straight to it.

        Difficulty

        Relaxed and Standard play it straight; Frantic tightens patience and spawn rates for a real test. Zen removes the losing condition entirely — rare weather, gentler traffic and a slower camera — for learning the tools, or just building, with no pressure.

        Tactics — a few opening moves

        • Start small. A tight loop with the right junction control consistently out-earns a sprawling maze of stop signs.
        • Upgrade before you expand. A junction or store upgrade often buys more throughput per dollar than another block of road.
        • Match the control to the load. Give-way for light traffic, a light once a junction gets busy both ways, a roundabout once it's genuinely busy.
        • Watch Trouble Spots. It's always up to date and always names your three worst junctions right now.
        • Keep a tow truck on standby. One hired car clears stuck or broken-down traffic before it backs up an entire street.
        • One-way a tight corridor. It costs nothing and can turn a two-lane headache into one smooth flow.
